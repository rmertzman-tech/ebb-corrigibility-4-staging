Before you begin

A note on privacy and purpose

What this tool is: Examined is a philosophical reflection tool supporting self-knowledge and values clarification. It is not a mental health service, not a substitute for therapy or counseling, and not designed for clinical use.

Your data stays in your browser: Your essays, profile, coherence readings, and conversation archive are stored only in this browser. Philosophy Lab Corporation has no database and cannot see what you write.

When you use the Companion, messages are transmitted: Each message travels via an encrypted connection to Anthropic's Claude API, which processes your message and a summary of your profile to generate a response. Anthropic states that retained API data is not used for model training without express permission; retention can depend on the service configuration.

Examined also looks at patterns across your check-ins over time — for example, if your readings have been declining, or if one area has stayed low for a while — and may adjust its tone or suggest Recovery Mode based on those patterns. You can always see why, and you can always tell it a pattern doesn't fit.

Write only what you are comfortable transmitting to a third-party AI service. If you prefer not to transmit, use Local Reflection mode in the Companion tab.

If you are in crisis: Examined is not monitored, cannot contact help for you, and does not automatically notify your instructor. If you or someone else may be in immediate danger, call 911. For suicidal crisis or emotional distress, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) rather than relying on this app.

This tool is designed for adults. If you are under 18, please seek a parent or guardian's guidance before using it.

You can review this in the About tab at any time.

Your philosophical profile

Two steps, fifteen minutes. First, six scenario questions — calibrated against the full 24-item PRF assessment — that map your moral architecture. Then, six questions about your life and how you became who you are. Together they generate a personal philosophical profile that describes not just your values but your specific way of inhabiting them.

The Examined app holds that you are a moral agent — a being with values, history, and commitments that are genuinely yours. It holds equally that everyone you encounter is the same kind of being. Self-examination and the recognition of others as self-examining beings are not separate tasks. They are the same project, approached from the inside.

Meta-Constructor Workshop

What are you running, and did you choose it?

Most of what shapes your reactions, habits, and roles was built into you before you were old enough to choose it — by people you watched, places that trained you, pressures you adapted to survive. Some of it still serves you well. Some of it doesn't. This is where you look at one specific pattern closely enough to decide, on purpose, whether to keep it or actively construct something different. Examined will ask questions here. It will not tell you what the pattern is, where it came from, or what to build instead — that judgment, and the reasoning behind it, has to be yours.

Step 1 of 3 — Name what's running
What pattern have you noticed?

Be specific — not "I get anxious," but "I go silent in meetings when a senior person disagrees with me."

Where do you think this was built?

Someone you watched? A place that trained you into it? Or is it genuinely yours — chosen, not inherited?

Step 2 of 3 — Keep or revise

Not everything inherited needs to change. Some patterns served the situation they were built for, and still serve you now.

Not a summary of the decision — the actual reasoning. What did you weigh?

Step 3 of 3 — Build the alternative
What would you construct instead?
Why this, specifically?

What makes this feel authentically yours to build — not what you think you're supposed to want, or what worked for someone else?

One small, real, checkable next step

Not a resolution. An actual move you can make once, soon.

When will you check whether this was real?

Good — deciding to keep something on purpose, after actually looking at it, is a real act of authorship. That's worth recording.

About Examined

Privacy, purpose & limits

What this tool is

Examined is a philosophical reflection tool. Its profiles and Companion interpretations are partial, revisable maps, not facts about who you really are. It is not a mental health service, not a substitute for therapy or counseling, and not designed for clinical use.

Patterns Examined notices over time

Examined looks at trends across your coherence check-ins — for example, a declining trajectory, or one area staying low for a while — and may adjust the Companion's tone or suggest Recovery Mode as a result. This is a pattern-recognition heuristic, not a diagnosis. You can always see what it noticed, in plain language, in the Coherence tab, and you can always tell it a pattern doesn't fit — doing so will quiet that specific suggestion for a period of time.

If you are in crisis

Examined is not monitored and cannot contact help for you. If you or someone else may be in immediate danger, call 911. For suicidal crisis or emotional distress, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, available 24/7 in the US).

For students

Your instructor cannot access your profile, essays, or Companion conversations unless you explicitly share them.
